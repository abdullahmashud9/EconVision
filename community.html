Academic & Policy Network

The EconVision Community

A collaborative, unpretentious circle connecting economics undergraduates, graduate scholars, college professors, and policy analysts to dissect papers, debug econometric code, critique thesis drafts, and share honest academic guidance.

How We Collaborate

What Actually Happens Inside EconVision

We started EconVision because economics can feel isolating when you are stuck debugging an econometric script or trying to figure out if your thesis idea has an endogeneity flaw. Here is what our members work on together every week.

Pre-print & Working Paper Reading Circles

Every fortnight, members pick a recent working paper from NBER, CEPR, or arXiv and dissect it line-by-line. We don't just read the abstract—we scrutinize the identification assumptions, the parallel trends tests in staggered DiD, and how the authors addressed unobserved confounders.

  • Causal inference identification reviews
  • Deconstructing AER & QJE replication code
  • Translating technical math into plain economic intuition

Undergraduate & Master's Thesis Clinic

Writing a thesis is difficult, especially if you attend a regional college without dedicated econometrics lab support. Members share early-stage research proposals, survey questionnaires, and regression outputs for constructive peer review before submitting to university advisors.

  • Literature review structuring & gap identification
  • Instrumental Variable (IV) validity checks
  • Fixing standard error clustering and panel unit roots

Data Wrangling & Code Troubleshooting

Applied economics requires messy data management. Our discussion channels are filled with active snippets helping peers merge NSSO consumer rounds, extract historical FRED series via API, clean NFHS survey weights, or debug R packages like fixest and did.

  • R, Stata, and Python script optimization
  • Census, WDI, and RBI DBIE data cleaning scripts
  • Event study and coefficient plotting best practices

Pre-Doc, Grad School & RA Career Guidance

Demystifying the economics career path. We share honest perspectives on applying for Pre-Doctoral Fellowships (PREDOC, J-PAL, NBER), cold emailing professors for research assistant roles, preparing for quantitative exams, and writing compelling Statements of Purpose (SOP).

  • SOP reviews by admitted PhDs and Pre-Docs
  • Math preparation roadmaps (Real Analysis & Linear Algebra)
  • Navigating funding and scholarships in India and abroad
Specialized Circles

Active Research & Discussion Working Groups

Members organize into specialized interest tracks to share literature preprints and collaborate on joint empirical working papers.

Working Group A

Applied Microeconomics & Development

Focuses on randomized evaluations (RCTs), health and nutrition interventions, education policy, rural credit deepening, and welfare scheme delivery across South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa.
Bi-weekly discussion threads
Working Group B

Macroeconomics, Central Banking & Financial Systems

Tracks inflation dynamics, monetary policy transmission (RBI / Fed / ECB), sovereign bond yields, banking sector asset quality (NPAs), and international capital flows.
Macro forecast & yield curve analyses
Working Group C

Causal Econometrics & Computational Methods

Dedicated to quasi-experimental research designs: modern staggered Difference-in-Differences, Regression Discontinuity (RDD), Synthetic Control, and Julia/Python dynamic modeling.
Replication code & methodological notes
Working Group D

Spatial Economics, Trade & Regional Growth

Investigates regional growth convergence among Indian states, urban agglomeration, port logistics, supply chain tariffs, and the political economy of industrial corridors.
State GSDP & trade data deep dives

Community Standards

The EconVision Code of Academic Ethos

To keep our community genuinely helpful, intellectually honest, and free from superficial noise, all members agree to uphold these core commitments:

  1. Zero Gatekeeping & Patient Mentorship: Beginners asking foundational questions—such as how to interpret a regression table, what an instrumental variable is, or how to import data into R—are welcomed with encouragement, clear explanations, and paper references, not condescension.
  2. High Signal Over Noise: No promotional spam, generic motivational quotes, or unverified stock market speculation. Every post should provide empirical data, a clear economic question, a working draft, or a verifiable paper summary.
  3. Critique the Methodology, Support the Author: Research improves through rigorous scrutiny. Point out potential endogeneity, omitted variable bias, or measurement error respectfully, always offering suggestions for alternative estimators or data sources.
  4. Commitment to Open Science & Replication: When sharing findings or models, provide replication code, equation definitions, and public data links so peers can reproduce the results independently.
