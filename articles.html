Economics Articles & Research Insights

Curated research summaries, econometric evaluations, and seminal policy papers authored by renowned economists from India and around the globe.

Macro & Finance ⏱️ 8 min read

Breaking the Twin Balance Sheet: Banking Reforms and India's Investment Cycle

By Dr. Arvind Subramanian & Dr. Raghuram Rajan
Brown University & University of Chicago Booth School of Business

An empirical investigation into India's corporate over-leverage and non-performing asset (NPA) clean-up from 2014 to 2024. The authors evaluate how the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) and capital adequacy mandates repaired bank lending balance sheets to support long-term private capital expenditure.

Key Finding
Bank recapitalization combined with strict asset quality reviews accelerated credit flow to mid-market manufacturing, though infrastructure risk resolution requires deeper domestic corporate bond markets.
RePEc Archive
Development & Welfare ⏱️ 10 min read

The Capability Approach & Social Provisioning: Re-examining Public Goods in South Asia

By Prof. Amartya Sen & Prof. Jean Drèze
Harvard University & Delhi School of Economics

Contrasting GDP-centric growth metrics with substantive human capabilities. The paper assesses India's public health investments, nutrition schemes (PDS & POSHAN), and primary schooling infrastructure, arguing that sustained economic expansion fundamentally depends on early-life human capital investments.

Key Finding
High growth rates do not automatically translate into improved social development without universal, non-discriminatory public delivery systems in primary healthcare and basic literacy.
Google Scholar
Inequality & Mobility ⏱️ 12 min read

The Opportunity Atlas: Intergenerational Mobility and Childhood Neighborhood Effects

By Prof. Raj Chetty, Nathaniel Hendren & John Friedman
Opportunity Insights, Harvard University & Brown University

Using anonymized longitudinal tax records for over 20 million children, this landmark study demonstrates that neighborhood environments during early childhood exert powerful causal effects on adult earnings, college attendance, and incarceration rates, creating micro-geographies of economic mobility.

Key Finding
Each additional year a child spends in a high-opportunity neighborhood increases adult income by ~4%, proving that place-based interventions and affordable housing vouchers have compounding lifelong returns.
Opportunity Insights
Experimental & RCTs ⏱️ 9 min read

Targeted Cash Transfers vs. In-Kind Food Aid: Randomized Evidence from Rural India

By Prof. Abhijit Banerjee & Prof. Esther Duflo
Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) - J-PAL

A multi-state randomized controlled trial across rural communities in West Bengal, Bihar, and Tamil Nadu comparing direct unconditional bank transfers with subsidized grain provisioning. Explores dietary diversity, agricultural reinvestment, and female household bargaining power.

Key Finding
Direct liquidity increased household consumption of micro-nutrients and protein without reducing adult labor supply, while mobile direct-benefit-transfers (DBT) significantly reduced leakage.
J-PAL Portal
Institutional Economics ⏱️ 11 min read

The Colonial Origins of Comparative Development: An Empirical Investigation

By Prof. Daron Acemoglu, Simon Johnson & James A. Robinson
MIT & University of Chicago (Nobel Laureates)

Using historic European settler mortality rates as an instrumental variable for contemporary institutions, this seminal paper proves that secure property rights and broad-based political institutions are the primary fundamental cause of long-run cross-country income differences.

Key Finding
Institutional quality differences explain over 70% of cross-country variance in per-capita income, demonstrating that extractive institutions persist and handicap economic growth across centuries.
AER Citation
Labor & Natural Exp ⏱️ 8 min read

Minimum Wages and Employment: The Empirical Credibility Revolution

By Prof. David Card & Prof. Alan B. Krueger
UC Berkeley & Princeton University

The foundational paper that ushered in modern quasi-experimental economics. Comparing fast-food restaurants in New Jersey following an 18% minimum wage increase with neighboring Pennsylvania as a control group, the authors found no evidence that moderate wage floors reduce low-wage employment.

Key Finding
Challenged textbook competitive labor market dogma, providing empirical evidence for labor monopsony power and establishing Difference-in-Differences as standard econometric practice.
NBER w4509
Wealth & Distribution ⏱️ 10 min read

Capital in the 21st Century: The Dynamics of $r > g$ and Global Wealth Accumulation

By Prof. Thomas Piketty & Prof. Gabriel Zucman
Paris School of Economics & UC Berkeley

Assembling three centuries of national wealth and tax data across 30 nations. The authors establish that when the private rate of return on capital ($r$) consistently exceeds the economic growth rate ($g$), inherited wealth outpaces earned income, driving extreme long-term inequality.

Key Finding
Without progressive wealth taxation and global tax transparency treaties, wealth-to-income ratios in 21st-century economies trend toward 19th-century patrimonial highs.
World Inequality Lab
Game Theory & Policy ⏱️ 7 min read

Strategic Games and Law: Beyond the Invisible Hand in Developing Economies

By Prof. Kaushik Basu
Cornell University & Former Chief Economist of the World Bank

A game-theoretic framework demonstrating that legal statutes cannot alter economic behavior unless they shift the focal equilibrium of market participants. Examines bureaucratic corruption, asymmetric bribery laws, and property contracts in emerging South Asian markets.

Key Finding
Legal enforcement is itself an endogenous game between citizens and police; effective policy requires altering strategic expectations rather than simply imposing top-down penalties.

Gender & Economic History ⏱️ 9 min read

The U-Shaped Female Labor Force Curve: Century of Gender, Careers, and Caregiving

By Prof. Claudia Goldin
Harvard University (Nobel Laureate in Economic Sciences)

A historical panel covering 200 years of female workforce participation. Goldin establishes the "U-shaped" curve where female labor participation first drops during agricultural-to-factory transitions before rising with white-collar education, the contraceptive pill, and service-sector growth.

Key Finding
The modern gender earnings gap is primarily driven by the "motherhood penalty" and workplace demands for long, non-linear hours rather than initial educational disparities.
NBER Archive
Trade & Global Order ⏱️ 8 min read

The Globalization Trilemma: Sovereignty, Democracy, and Hyperglobalization

By Prof. Dani Rodrik
John F. Kennedy School of Government, Harvard University

Formulates the political trilemma of the world economy: nations cannot simultaneously maintain national sovereignty, democratic politics, and hyper-global economic integration. Explores the resurgence of industrial policy, supply chain security, and strategic tariffs in the post-Washington Consensus era.

Key Finding
To preserve domestic social safety nets and democratic legitimacy, international trade regimes must allow countries policy space for targeted industrial subsidies and labor safeguards.
Google Scholar
Digital Rails & Inclusion ⏱️ 7 min read

Digital Payment Rails (UPI) and Rural Credit Access in Northeast India

By Abdullah Mashud
Economics Scholar, Darrang College

An empirical study on the expansion of Unified Payments Interface (UPI) micro-transactions across informal retail vendors and agricultural households in Assam. Evaluates how digital cashflow footprints reduce collateral requirements for micro-credit disbursements from rural regional banks.

Key Finding
Adoption of QR-based digital payments increased average monthly formal loan approvals by 28% among micro-enterprises by converting previously unrecorded cash turnover into verified transaction history.
EconVision Working Paper
Spatial Economics & Growth ⏱️ 9 min read

Spatial Disparities & Regional Growth Convergence Across Indian States

By Dr. Bilal Ahmad Bhat
Assistant Professor (Economics), Azim Premji University

Using dynamic panel data models over three decades of State Domestic Product (GSDP), this research analyzes sigma and beta convergence among Indian states, investigating why Southern and Western coastal hubs diverge steadily from Northern hinterland states in manufacturing density and tax revenue mobilization.

Key Finding
Interstate fiscal equalization transfers have mitigated consumption inequality, but productive capital convergence requires aggressive logistics corridors, power infrastructure, and localized governance reforms.
EconVision Working Paper
Commodity Shocks & Africa ⏱️ 8 min read

Terms-of-Trade Volatility and Sovereign Fiscal Buffers in West Africa

By Emmanuel T Koduah
Economics Scholar, University of Cape Coast (Ghana)

A structural Vector Autoregression (SVAR) analysis evaluating the transmission of global gold and cocoa price fluctuations to sovereign debt yields, domestic inflation, and foreign exchange reserves in Ghana and the ECOWAS trade corridor.

Key Finding
Rules-based sovereign stabilization funds combined with domestic currency bond issuance reduce exchange rate pass-through to headline consumer inflation during commodity price downswings.
EconVision Working Paper
Innovation & State ⏱️ 9 min read

The Entrepreneurial State: Public R&D and Mission-Oriented Innovation

By Prof. Mariana Mazzucato
Institute for Innovation and Public Purpose, University College London (UCL)

Challenging the conventional narrative that the private sector is the sole driver of innovation. Mazzucato traces the historical development of foundational digital technologies (the internet, GPS, touchscreens, algorithms, and mRNA vaccines) back to bold, patient public investment by state research agencies.

Key Finding
Governments must transition from merely fixing market failures to actively co-creating and shaping markets through mission-oriented industrial strategies that retain public equity in technological returns.
