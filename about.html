Institutional Overview

About EconVision

Fostering academic excellence, empirical rigor, and collaborative scholarship across the economics discipline.

Our Mission

Uniting Economic Minds Beyond Institutional Boundaries

EconVision was established to address a persistent gap in the economics ecosystem: while groundbreaking research occurs daily in universities, think tanks, and central banks, opportunities for organic, cross-hierarchical dialogue between students, early-career researchers, and established faculty remain rare.

By building an accessible, intellectually rigorous community centered around our LinkedIn group, we provide an ongoing scholarly forum where ideas are debated, methodologies are refined, and mentorship happens naturally.

"Our vision is an economics discipline where curiosity is welcomed, empirical standards are upheld, and every student has access to guidance from leading minds."
Academic Ethos

Core Tenets of Our Forum

  • 1. Methodological Rigor We prioritize evidence-based claims, replicable econometrics, and sound theoretical grounding.
  • 2. Scholarly Civility Debate is welcomed; ad hominem is prohibited. We treat differing schools of thought with academic respect.
  • 3. Open Access to Mentorship Democratizing guidance for graduate school applications, research assistantships, and publishing.
Audience & Scope

Who Participates in EconVision?

Undergraduate & PhD candidates, university faculty, economic consultants, central bank analysts, development economists, and financial researchers across the globe.

Our Principles

The Four Pillars of EconVision

Pillar I

Empirical Rigor

Encouraging transparency, open datasets, and replicable econometric techniques in all research discussions.
Pillar II

Scholarly Equity

Ensuring equal access to resources, career guidance, and academic networks regardless of university affiliation or geography.
Pillar III

Policy Relevance

Bridging the gap between mathematical abstractions and the pressing socio-economic challenges of our era.
Pillar IV

Mentorship Culture

Facilitating meaningful connections between experienced professors and aspiring young economists.
